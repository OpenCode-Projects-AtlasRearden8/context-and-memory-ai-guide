# When to Start a Fresh Chat

---

Here's a secret about people who use AI well: they start a **lot** of chats. Not because they're chaotic — because they've learned that a clean whiteboard beats a smudged one, every time.

A fresh chat is not "losing your work." It's a reset button for the thing that actually matters: **the quality of the AI's attention.**

## The signs it's time

You don't need a hard rule about message counts. Watch for these:

1. **You've started saying "as I mentioned earlier…" or "remember when I said…"** — you're re-explaining inside a chat, which means the chat has already lost the thread.
2. **The topic has shifted more than once.** A chat about your website became a chat about invoicing and is now about your eBay listing photos. That's three topics sharing one smudged whiteboard.
3. **Answers have gone generic.** The AI stopped referencing your specifics and started giving "in general…" advice. (Full disease description in Chapter 7.)
4. **The chat contains a big pile of pasted documents you're done with.** They're still on the whiteboard, still eating attention.
5. **Things feel slower or off and you can't say why.** A vague "this chat feels heavy" is usually real.
6. **You're about to start a new task.** Honestly, this alone can justify a fresh start — and it's the pro move.

## The counterarguments — let's grade them fairly

**"But my chat has all the context!"**
Part of that context is a *pile of finished old discussions* that no longer help, and part is *still-relevant critical facts*. Fresh chat + pasting a summary keeps only the second kind. That trade is almost always a win. (The summary technique is Chapter 5.)

**"But starting over means re-explaining everything."**
You'll re-explain either way — either by typing "as I said before…" into the smudged chat, or by pasting an 8-line summary into a fresh one. The second one is faster, and the AI does *much* better with it.

**"But I want a record!"**
Keep the old chat! Nobody's deleting it. But treat it as your **archive**, not your **workbench**. You wouldn't write the next chapter of your book on top of a sticky-note pile from last month.

## The pro move: closing a chat deliberately

When a good chat is ending, spend one message. Ask:

> **"Before we finish: summarize everything we decided and figured out that would matter in a future conversation — as a compact handoff note in my voice: my project, my goals, our decisions, open questions, and any constraints. Bullet points only, under 250 words."**

That's your handoff note. Copy it into your notes app (or wherever you keep project notes). Next task about that project? **New chat, paste the handoff note at the top, work.** You've just carried forward the good 20% and left behind the noise.

Three rules for handoff notes:

1. **In your voice, but compact.** Bullet points. The AI doesn't need your whole conversation history — it needs the *state* of your project.
2. **Name constraints explicitly.** "Don't use markdown in output," "sound like a friend, not a consultant," "I'm on the free plan, keep answers short." These are the first rules old chats forget, so make them the first lines of the new one.
3. **Verify before you use it.** Read the note the AI wrote. If it left out the thing that matters most, add it yourself before pasting. *You're* the boss of context — the tool just drafts it.

## When NOT to start fresh

Fresh chats aren't automatically better. Keep going in the same chat when:

- You're **mid-task and flowing well** — refinements, iteration, "make this tighter." Shared live context is exactly what helps here.
- The conversation is **short** — under a dozen messages, no docs, no drift. Nothing to reset.
- You're giving **feedback on something the AI just produced** — "good, but turn that paragraph into bullets" only works in the chat that produced it.

## Try this

1. Look at the chat you use *most*. Count how many assigned topics it covers. (It's probably more than one.) Consider a heavier topic deserving of a fresh, focused chat — with a handoff note pasted in.
2. Do the one-message closing ritual on your next good chat: the "compact handoff note" prompt. Save the note.
3. Next confusing long chat, instead of scrolling back to find what you said, ask directly: **"What would a new chat not know that we've covered here? List just the important stuff."** Then start fresh with those bullets.
